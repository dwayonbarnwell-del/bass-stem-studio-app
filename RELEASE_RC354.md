# RC354: rehearsal alternatives that follow each chord change

Rehearsal previously ranked alternatives mainly by genre and rhythm, copied a source bar's pitch offsets over the song and retargeted a transition's final cluster. A correct genre could therefore still produce notes that clashed with the song's chord quality or landed poorly.

The new rehearsal harmony layer fits alternatives to the actual saved chords, key, slash bass and beat grid. Strong notes follow chord tones; passing notes follow the current tonal context, with brief resolving chromatic approaches retained. Landings use the destination bass note, and adapted voicings avoid unnecessary jumps beyond an octave. This changes rehearsal adaptations, not the original source studies or the song's saved harmony.

The change selector offers the whole progression or an individual movement actually present in the song, such as 1→6. Matching source intervals rank first in Basslines, Runs and Fills. Focusing a change applies its replacement to that movement, and changing genre or movement retains the selected study type. Short passages without room for a complete run or fill are omitted from focused suggestions.

Whole sequences such as 1–4–5–4 follow every actual chord, including the return at the loop boundary. Captured source harmony is not presented as verified. All previous genres, source families, source IDs, deletions and recordings remain. Existing source labels and the 100/50/50 practice defaults remain; Rehearsal retains guide 100% and music 50%.

Note View now shows numbered progression steps, the approaching change and beat countdown, labels at the actual change times, and highlighted landing notes. It uses the existing key-numbering preference, including relative-major numbering in minor keys. Unknown keys show chord names rather than guessed numbers. Fills and runs start on the On changes setting; Every bar remains available.

Lead-ins use the saved beat positions, including variable timing and changes between beats. Notes remain inside their current chord window, genuine quiet passages stay clear and slash chords retain the specified bass. Choice changes stay on the existing audio-clock transport and apply at the next loop boundary.

On the next play, corrected song keys, chords, beat timing or bass notes invalidate the cached guide so it cannot keep using the previous analysis.

Validation:

- Sixty alternatives fitted across twenty populated genres.
- All 42 directed degree changes in all 12 major and minor keys for representative bassline, run and fill choices: 3,024 fits.
- Checked chord-tone agreement, minor/diminished/suspended/seventh qualities, correct root or slash-bass landings, bounded register changes, no overlaps, full 1–4–5–4 sequences, focused 1→6 choices and preserved saved harmony.
- Checked unknown-key labels, minor relative-major numbering, quiet passages, uncertain chords, changes between beats, variable beat timing and brief resolving chromatic notes.
- Real mobile browser playback checked numbered change options, the approach and landing display, the A landing for C→Am, retained Runs selection and uninterrupted movement/genre changes.
- Existing rehearsal and recorded mixer/source controls checked.

Suggestions depend on the song's saved key and chord analysis being correct. Source transcription and separation remain estimates. These checks establish the stated timing and harmony rules; they are not a claim that every adaptation has been manually auditioned.
