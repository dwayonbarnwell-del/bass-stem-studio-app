# RC293: genre-matched practice band

Chords and drums start enabled. Auto accompaniment is the new default; the old globally saved pocket beat no longer overrides every study. Explicit new manual choices, volumes and sound toggles are remembered. “Use genre settings” restores all four selectors to Auto without changing volume or sound toggles.

Each study selects its own drum rhythm, acoustic kit voicing, keyboard rhythm and keyboard sound. Seventeen distinct drum patterns and seventeen keyboard arrangements cover worship, gospel, praise, soul, jazz, blues, fusion, reggae, highlife, afrobeat, makossa, soukous/seben, mbalax, mbaqanga, sungura, soca and zouk. Specific styles take priority over broad descriptions such as praise. These are original practice arrangements informed by genre characteristics, not artist transcriptions or complete traditional percussion ensembles. Genre inspiration: Tosin Aribisala's African Rhythms Applied to the Drumset (Drumeo), Gil Sharone's one-drop lessons (Drumeo), and Edd Bateman / Jeannot Bel's African ensemble and Central African guitar lessons (World Music Method).

Keyboard voicings use the existing chord pitch classes, with short voice-leading distances and MIDI 48–79 above the bass. Each note ends by the next harmonic change. Bass notes, keys, progression combinations and original harmony remain unchanged. Four-bar rhythm positions, alternating sample hits and intentional velocity accents provide variation without random timing. Open hats close on the following hat strike. Bass, drums and keys still share one native audio-clock loop; volume and mute changes do not restart it.

The existing Salamander Grand piano remains. New locally hosted sounds are Greg Sullivan's recorded Wurlitzer EP200 (two dynamic layers, CC BY 3.0) and FreePats' drawbar organ sampled from the setBfree emulation (CC0). The organ is not represented as a physical Hammond recording. The 26 WAVs total 4,755,340 bytes; original sustain loops, source tuning and relative sample levels are retained. Full provenance, checksums and licences are in audio/keys-rc293. No uploaded installer audio was extracted or redistributed. Library and available prior-chat context identify ear-training-101-v-4-d-installer.exe; they do not establish a separate reusable keyboard sample library.

The ten Basslines menus now contain both patterns and recordings under the relevant genre: Worship; Gospel & Soul; Praise; Reggae; Highlife & Afrobeat; Makossa; Soukous & Seben; Other African styles; Soca & Zouk; My progressions & Unsorted. All 107 generated studies and 72 recordings remain reachable exactly once. Unconfirmed recordings stay Unsorted rather than receiving an invented genre. Custom progression IDs, saved deletions, original recording playback, excerpt/speed/transpose controls and all 200 runs/fills are retained. Additional drums/keys are not layered onto recordings without verified beat grids.
