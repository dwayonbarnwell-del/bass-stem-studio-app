# RC361: separate voices, editable keys and a clearer mixer

Ear Training starts with Bass 100%, Drums 21%, Others 7% and Voice 21%. Voice was not given a separate starting level in the request; 21% is used. The four faders and mute states are independent. New mix defaults migrate once, retaining sound choices and later adjustments. Recordings have separate vocal controls; instrumental practice patterns show a disabled vocal control rather than inventing a voice track.

Recorded practice uses the existing browser separation model's four outputs, retains the previously isolated bass, and reconstructs the other-instrument residual from the original mix. Rehearsal reuses saved bass/vocal separation, dividing the remaining instrumental backing into drums and other instruments. Saved project audio and analysis remain unchanged. Derived vocal/drum caches are bounded to 96 MiB and do not replace song-library data. If new vocal preparation is unavailable, existing six-channel recordings can still play, with Voice disabled and a short status.

The key control works in recorded and generated Basslines, Runs, Fills and Rehearsal. It transposes audio, displayed notes and harmony together while preserving the source clock and tempo. Drums retain their pitch. Source mode is retained; changing tonic does not falsely turn a major recording into a minor recording. Rehearsal guide notes may move by octaves to stay in the supported bass register, without editing the source transcription. SoundTouchJS core is locally hosted with its licence and pinned source reference. The original-key path is sample-exact apart from its shared processing delay; that delay is included in the visual clock.

Quiet stems are normalised before the user's faders. A shared look-ahead limiter controls combined peaks without automatically undoing reduced or muted faders. Source replacement, live key/style changes, pause/resume and multiple selected repeat sections retain their audio clock. Prepared original vocals remain separate from other instruments.

Key, BPM and Original/Reset are three equal 44px cards matching the playback row. BPM and timing values are centred. Others, Drums and Voice are three equal mute cards, with speaker icons and full accessible names. Key captions use short names such as Key: Ab Maj. Redundant recorded-mix text and bottom BPM are removed. Keyboard labels and selected-source details are shortened, while full provenance stays in option details. The large fret views and compact playback layout remain.

Validation covers actual processed waveforms at 20 bass/vocal pitches and key shifts, unchanged duration and attack windows, stereo phase, sample-exact original-key playback, independent gain/mute nodes, quiet-audio gain, silence and peak limiting. Rehearsal controller checks cover separated stems, live key changes, guide register, muted-slider edits, pause/resume and multi-section repeat. Existing harmony, phrase partition, note-role, naming, deletion, cultural-source and bass-register checks pass.

Browser checks used a real Rockaway excerpt: vocals alone produced audio, muting Voice produced silence with the other faders at zero, and changing Ab to Bb moved both audio and displayed notes while retaining the 11.504-second loop. A 7:55.544 rehearsal retained its full source clock through a key change and a Makossa alternative. Equal cards, centred inputs, hidden sliders, timeline repeat selection and both tall fret views were checked on phone layouts. First-use vocal preparation is asynchronous and may take time; successful results are cached.

The old recording_practice_rc331.cjs fixture fails before exercising playback because it omits BassRecordedPhrases required by the unchanged current catalogue loader. Current recording/cultural-source, label and audio checks are used for this release. No retired recordings, genres, stable IDs or deletion rules are changed.
